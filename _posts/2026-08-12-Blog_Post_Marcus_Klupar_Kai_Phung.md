 **From the Far Reaches of Alaska to the Far Reaches of Space**

 Many of us spend our lives in a single state or city, but Marcuc Klupar, in his own words, “grew up everywhere.” Part of his childhood was in Southeast Alaska; he went to high school in Philadelphia, attended undergraduate school in Missouri, and now attends graduate school in Arizona. In my interview with him, it was especially interesting to learn about his hometown in Skagway, Alaska, which is just above Juneau. I had to ask about the salmon fishing, and he shared that salmon typically don't spawn there. There were hatcheries when he was a kid, but they stopped them in 2015, which meant very few salmon came back naturally to spawn. You should also know that it is a journey in and of itself to make it to Skagway. Marcus usually flies into Juneau and then takes a plane or a ferry, and most of us do not realize the extent of ferries in Alaska. Marcus shared about it like this: “Flying is risky sometimes, so everybody takes the ferries. The ferry from Juneau to Skagway is a seven-hour trip, and for other places, you might be on the ferry for three or four days. It's like the public transportation system.” Growing up in Alaska shaped much of Marcus’ character as he was constantly surrounded by nature, which grounded him in his curiosity towards the processes of life.

 After graduating from high school, Marcus’ journey into space exploration began at St. Louis University, a Jesuit university. He chose an undergraduate degree in aerospace engineering, and luckily, he really clicked with the field from the beginning. His interest in the field led him to pursue further studies at the University of Arizona, which has incredible programs in space exploration.

 **Pointed Efforts to See the Universe with Control Systems**

 *“The telescope is similar; it wants to stay pointed at zero degrees despite outside factors wanting to change that, so we manage that with control systems.”* -Marcus Klupar

 Since the beginning of Marcus’ work at the University of Arizona (UA), his focus has been on control systems. Marcus explained his work to me in this simple-to-understand manner: “You could have any kind of system, like your car or your HVAC heating and ventilation systems; they all use control.”

 In his master's at the UA, he worked on thermal control, but for his PhD, he switched to wavefront sensing and control. Specifically, he works on the pointing team at the University of Arizona Space Astrophysics Lab (UASAL). Marcus shared, “Our job is to ensure the telescope is looking at the right target. A telescope has a bore angle, which is like looking down the barrel of a gun; you want to point it precisely at a star to do science or detect exoplanets. External vibrations, momentum, and spinning wheels cause vibrations that make it hard to point exactly. The pointing team takes all these dynamics into account to ensure the telescope bore angle is precisely aligned with the center of the star over time.”

 Marcus brought back an analogy to help me understand what this looks like in a control system we benefit from every day: “The best analogy is an AC unit in your house. In Tucson, it can be 110 degrees outside, but you can set your AC to 80 degrees. Even as the temperature changes outside, the temperature inside stays constant because a control system determines the correct inputs for the HVAC to keep the house cool. The telescope is similar; it wants to stay pointed at zero degrees despite outside factors wanting to change that, so we manage that with control systems.”


This work is critical to the mission of the telescopes because if your telescope isn't pointing where you want it to, the science images won't be good. 

**Finding Meaning and Purpose in Astronomy**

*"Everybody is curious about where they come from... This work is how I choose to give myself meaning and purpose."* -Marcus

When I asked Marcus about his drive for space exploration, he shared, “I think I'm just a naturally curious person, and it bothers me when someone doesn't know the answer to a question. When you ask good, important questions, you get good, important answers.” Many individuals interested in space exploration would agree that they share this deep curiosity for exploration and answering questions. 

Marcus continued to share his motivation: “Everybody is curious about where they come from. Having an answer to that would be an amazing thing to help us know the universe and our place in it better. When I was 18, I had a bit of a crisis concerning my purpose in life and whether anything had meaning. Going through that experience taught me that you give yourself meaning and purpose. This work is how I choose to do it.”

Meaning and purpose come to each of us in different ways. For Marcus, it has been through aiding in astronomical work that brings new scientific understanding and could one day help us identify other Earth-like planets.
